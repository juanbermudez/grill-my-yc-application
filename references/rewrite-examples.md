# Rewrite calibration

All companies, figures, and statements in these examples are fictional. They illustrate the skill's behavior and are not claims about any real applicant or the historical companies. No outcome measurement is implied unless explicitly supplied in the example's facts.

## 1. Product: replace a category cloud with an action

**Question:** What does your company do?

**Draft and facts:** “We are an innovative AI-native platform revolutionizing accounts receivable for service businesses.” The product reads invoices and drafts payment reminders. An accountant approves every reminder before sending.

**Rewrite:** “We read service businesses' invoices and draft payment reminders for their accountants to approve and send.”

**Why:** Identifies the customer, action, and human boundary. Does not invent saved time, collections improvement, or autonomous sending. The draft's adjectives supplied none of those facts.

## 2. Traction: keep customers, pilots, and waitlists separate

**Question:** How far along are you?

**Facts:** As of October 19, 2026, two companies pay $400 each per month; three companies have unpaid pilots; 120 people are on a waitlist.

**Rewrite:** “As of October 19, 2026, two companies pay $400 each per month ($800 MRR). Three more are running unpaid pilots.”

**Why:** The waitlist can be omitted here because actual payment and use are stronger evidence. If asked for all demand or signup counts, include it with the correct label. Never write “125 customers” or “$9,600 in annual revenue”; $9,600 would be an annualized run rate, not revenue already earned.

## 3. Missing evidence: preserve an honest unknown

**Question:** How much time do you save customers?

**Facts:** Two pilot users say reporting feels faster. No before/after measurement exists.

**Rewrite:** “We have not measured time saved. Two pilot users say reporting is faster.”

**Weak point:** This supports a qualitative observation, not a numerical productivity claim.

**Follow-up, if reviewing:** “How long did the same reporting task take before and after?”

## 4. Insight: replace “easy to use” with a supported mechanism

**Question:** What do you understand that competitors don't?

**Draft:** “Existing products are clunky. Ours is simple and intuitive.”

**Additional facts:** In eight interviews, solo contractors said they record job details in text messages and will not retype them into a CRM. The prototype turns a forwarded message into a draft job record.

**Rewrite:** “The eight solo contractors we interviewed keep job details in text messages and refuse to enter them again in a CRM. Our prototype turns a forwarded message into a draft job record.”

**Why:** Gives an observed behavior and the product response. Does not claim that every contractor behaves this way or that competitors cannot add the feature.

## 5. Founder: use an accomplishment

**Question:** What is something impressive you have built?

**Draft and facts:** “I am a relentlessly resourceful, customer-obsessed engineer.” The founder built a scheduling app used weekly by 240 tutors while in college; no revenue information is supplied.

**Rewrite:** “In college, I built a scheduling app that 240 tutors used weekly.”

**Why:** The accomplishment carries the claim. Do not invent a launch duration, paying customer count, or revenue.

## 6. Conflicting facts: ask before polishing a false number

**Input:** One answer says seven paying companies; another says five. There is no date or explanation for the difference.

**Correct response:** “The application gives two paying-customer counts: seven and five. Which is current, and as of what date?”

Continue improving unrelated answers. Do not average the numbers, choose the more impressive one, or silently classify two as pilots.

## 7. A field limit: count the actual text

**Question:** Describe the company in 50 characters or fewer.

**Facts:** It is software that schedules shifts for dental practices.

**Answer:** “Shift scheduling for dental practices”

This is 37 characters including spaces, excluding the quotation marks. If the user supplies a different limit, validate the actual answer against that limit. Do not treat this historical field length as the rule for every question.

## 8. Grilling: follow the evidence rather than the script

**Founder:** “We have 300 customers and strong retention.”

**Coach:** “How many of the 300 paid in the last full month?”

**Founder:** “Six. The other 294 signed up free. We haven't measured retention.”

**Coach:** “Then describe six paying customers and 294 free signups. What evidence supports the retention claim?”

**Founder:** “None yet.”

**Revised answer:** “Six customers paid last month. We also have 294 free signups and have not measured retention.”

**Why:** The follow-up exposes the unsupported claim. Stop asserting retention; do not manufacture a cohort, describe this as zero retention, or use the admission process as a threat.
